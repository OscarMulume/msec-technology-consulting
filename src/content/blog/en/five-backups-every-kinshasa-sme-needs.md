Backups are often treated as a distant technical task. For an SME, they are above all a question of operational survival: without sales, receivables or customer contact details, the business can be blocked for a long time. Here are five families of data to protect, with a simple method for each.

## 1. Sales and stock

Sales, products, prices and stock movements are the heart of the business. The backup must be automatic, frequent and linked to a copy kept somewhere other than the point-of-sale hard drive.

Check that restoration preserves the links between a sale, its lines and its payments. A database that is backed up but cannot be restored correctly does not protect the business.

## 2. Accounting and supporting documents

Accounting records, invoices, receipts and payment evidence must be kept together. A copy of the figures alone, without the documents, makes audits difficult.

Organise files by period and type, with readable names. Avoid leaving the only copies in an employee's mailbox.

## 3. Company documents

Contracts, leases, insurance, tax documents, internal procedures and supplier contact details must be accessible even if the office is closed or a computer fails. An off-site copy protected by a password reduces this risk.

## 4. Equipment configuration

Routers, firewalls, access points, cameras and servers have a configuration that took work to create. Export these configurations regularly and keep them with the network documentation. When equipment is replaced, restoration is much faster.

## 5. Access and procedures

Passwords, access keys and recovery procedures must not depend on one person's memory. Use a password manager and define who can open backups. Do not store secrets in plain text in a shared file.

## Verify, do not just back up

A useful backup follows a few principles:

- several copies, on different media, including one off site;
- a frequency adapted to sales volume;
- encryption for sensitive copies;
- a responsible person and a written procedure;
- a regular restoration test.

Testing is the step most often forgotten. Choose a test file or database, restore it and check that the data is complete. Record the test date and result.

## Start simply

An SME does not need a complex system to make progress. Start with sales and accounting, add an off-site copy, then test a restoration. Repeat the operation regularly and let the plan evolve with the business.

A backup is not a product installed once. It is a controlled habit.
